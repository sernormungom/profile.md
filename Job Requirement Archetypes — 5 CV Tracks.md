# Job Requirement Archetypes — 5 CV Tracks

Oct 20, 2026 · @Norberto

## What this is built from

The applications page lists 33 applications. Behind them sit **25 distinct job ads** — several were submitted under more than one consultant profile. This document is built from the full ad text of all 25, not from their titles.

That distinction turned out to matter. Three of the 25 carry titles that point at the wrong job entirely: a "Senior Embedded Software Application Engineer" ad that is about LLM pipelines, a "Software Architect" ad that is about Microsoft 365 governance. Clustering by title would have put them in the wrong CV and produced a document arguing the wrong case.

The output is five **archetypes** — a composite job description per category, assembled from what the ads actually demand, with every requirement weighted by how many ads ask for it. You fill each archetype against your own experience; what survives becomes the CV.

## The requirement model

A flat list of skills is useless for writing a CV, because it does not say which items screen you out, which items you compete on, and which items are free to add. So every archetype below is described in six layers, and every requirement line carries two extra attributes.

### The six layers

| Layer | What it holds | What it decides in the CV |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 · Conditions | Location, work mode, contract form, duration, travel, language, licences | Whether to apply at all |
| 1 · Gates | Binary screen-outs: years, degree, a named tool, domain exposure | Must be visible in the top third of page one |
| 2 · Core | What the role does daily; asked by most ads in the category | The spine — every assignment entry argues one of these |
| 3 · Differentiators | "Meriterande" / nice-to-have; tie-breakers between shortlisted candidates | One line each, only if true |
| 4 · Domain & process | Standards, methodology, toolchain ecosystem | Named explicitly, never implied |
| 5 · Behaviour | How the ad describes the person | Shown through what assignments you took, not adjectives |

Layer 5 is the one normally thrown away. In these ads it is not decoration: the senior and consulting posts spend 30–40% of their text on it, and it is where "integrate deliveries from others" and "influence without formal personnel responsibility" live.

### The two attributes on every line

**Weight** — how many ads in that category ask for it, as *n*/*N*. A requirement marked "must" in one ad out of six is not a must for the archetype. This is what stops a CV being rewritten around an outlier.

**Proof** — what evidence the CV has to carry. Four grades:

1. **Named** — it appears in a skills list. Nearly worthless above seven years of experience.
2. **Applied** — a specific assignment where you used it, with the context.
3. **Owned** — you were accountable for the outcome it produced.
4. **Taught** — you set the standard, introduced the practice, or trained others in it.

Senior ads are implicitly asking for Owned or Taught and will read Named as absence. "AUTOSAR" in a skills list reads as exposure; "configured and integrated AUTOSAR Classic across three ECU variants, owned the integration toward the supplier" reads as the thing they are buying.

### Anti-signals

Each archetype also lists what gets a CV dropped. These are not the inverse of the requirements — they are the specific patterns these ads react badly to, like cloud experience listed without a named service, or Qt listed without a shipped user interface.

### The title traps

Three ads would have been filed wrongly on title alone, and all three are instructive about how these clients name roles.

**Volvo VOLGJP00043496, "SE\_Embedded Software Application Engineer\_Senior"** contains no embedded software at all. It asks for an MSc in Data Science, excellent Python, experience with Large Language Models, and the job is building pipelines that automate engineering tasks. The title is a slot in Volvo's staffing system, not a description. At least four of the Volvo ads share boilerplate paragraphs under different numbers, so the posting title tells you almost nothing — the "Description:" body is the real ad.

**Volvo Cars VOLVCAJP00002119, "Software Architect (Senior Professional)"** is a Microsoft 365 governance and Copilot-enablement assignment: SharePoint, Teams, data classification, access control. No code. This is the excluded outlier — one ad, no cluster, and a CV for it would share nothing with the other four.

**WirelessCar, "Senior Automotive Embedded Software Expert"** is not a development post. It is an advisory assignment: assess WirelessCar's gap toward embedded development, define competencies and organisation, recommend build-versus-buy, propose a roadmap. The deliverable is a report. It sits in B because the knowledge required is B's, but the CV framing for it is consulting, not engineering.

## Archetype A — Automotive Function Developer / Model-Based Control

**Built from 6 ads:** Volvo IC Tech (SI ICE control), Volvo Technology (thermal management), Volvo (electrohydraulic steering), Volvo (Expert, generic), Aurobay (thermal, xEV), Geely (body & comfort).

**One-line pitch the CV must make:** *I own a vehicle function from requirement breakdown to calibration in the vehicle, and I do it model-based.*

### Layer 0 · Conditions

Göteborg, onsite, permanent-team-shaped consulting. English is the working language across sites (Bangalore, Greensboro, Lyon, China). Geely adds 3–4 trips to China a year. Three of the six name a driving licence — B for Aurobay, C/CE truck licence as meritorious for the Volvo truck roles, plus T1/T2 Hällered test-track licence.

### Layer 1 · Gates

| Gate | Weight | Proof needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8+ years relevant automotive/embedded experience (12+ for the IC Tech Expert post, 7+ for Geely) | 5/6 | Years visible without arithmetic |
| M.Sc. in Mechatronics, Control Engineering, Electrical/Electronics, or Computer Engineering | 5/6 | Named degree and field |
| MATLAB / Simulink | 5/6 | Applied, on a shipped function |
| Prior automotive industry experience | 6/6 | Named OEM or Tier-1 |
| A named vehicle domain of specialisation | 6/6 | Owned — one domain, deeply |

The domain gate is the one most often missed. Every ad asks you to *specialise*: thermal management, steering, body/comfort, combustion and air-path, powertrain control, diagnostics. A CV that spreads thinly across all of them reads as none of them.

### Layer 2 · Core

| Requirement | Weight | Proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full V-cycle on a function: requirement breakdown, design, documentation, implementation, test, verification | 6/6 | Owned |
| Model-based development with Simulink plus production code generation (TargetLink and/or Embedded Coder) | 4/6 | Owned |
| ECU function development with calibratable parameters | 5/6 | Owned |
| Calibration and tuning on rig and in vehicle | 4/6 | Applied |
| SIL / MIL / HIL testing | 4/6 | Applied |
| Unit tests at both function-requirement and function-area level | 2/6 | Applied — Aurobay makes this its sharpest ask |
| AUTOSAR Classic integration, Vector DaVinci | 3/6 | Applied |
| Diagnostics and UDS protocol | 2/6 | Applied |
| CAN, CANoe, CANalyzer | 3/6 | Applied |
| Requirements tooling: SystemWeaver (SE-TOOL), Elektra, Jira, SWIM | 3/6 | Named is acceptable here |
| ISO 26262, at least working knowledge | 3/6 | Applied |
| Lead system engineering in a technical team; integrate deliveries from others | 4/6 | Owned |
| Supplier follow-up and technical interface toward suppliers | 2/6 | Owned |

### Layer 3 · Differentiators

CI/CD and automation of the development process itself — the Volvo Expert ad calls out pipelines and "automation of process steps" explicitly, and IC Tech builds half its assignment around automated development, automated testing and automated calibration. GitHub Copilot as a normal part of the way of working (Aurobay, stated as a plus). Physics-informed or hybrid AI/ML applied to control, diagnostics and calibration (IC Tech, extensively). Hydrogen or natural-gas spark-ignited heavy-duty engines. Prior Volvo Group or Geely toolchain experience. Static code analysis and MISRA. Thought leadership: technical roadmaps, patents, publications, standardisation work.

### Layer 4 · Domain & process

ISO 26262, SAE/ISO 21434, agile SAFe, the V-model. Toolchain ecosystem: MATLAB/Simulink, TargetLink, Embedded Coder, dSPACE rapid prototyping, ETAS INCA, ETAS ASCMO, ATI Vision, AVL Concerto, Jenkins, Git, SystemWeaver.

### Layer 5 · Behaviour

The Volvo ads repeat a specific profile almost verbatim: a T-shaped specialist who networks across sites, influences work at other locations, mentors and coaches, is a "role model of cutting-edge thinking", and can articulate value created beyond their own part of the organisation. IC Tech adds: challenges established approaches, decides across organisational boundaries, builds networks, follows external technology trends. None of this is filler — at Specialist and Expert grade it is the difference between the levels.

### Anti-signals

A general software CV with no Simulink and no vehicle. Function development claimed with no calibration or rig/vehicle testing anywhere. Breadth across six vehicle domains with depth in none. Listing ISO 26262 without ever naming an ASIL or a safety requirement you worked to.

## Archetype B — Embedded C++ Platform & Application Engineer

**Built from 7 ads:** Aliant (tech lead / architect), A-Society (AEB, Euro NCAP), ECARX (Android & QNX), Volvo (ADAS sensor fusion), Volvo Cars (Active Suspension & TPMS), Volvo Cars (Central Execution Environment DevOps), WirelessCar (embedded capability assessment).

**One-line pitch:** *I write production C++ on a vehicle platform, and I own the architecture and the quality bar around it.*

Where A is model-based and function-shaped, B is hand-written C++ on Linux, QNX or Android, close to the platform. The two share a domain and almost nothing else in daily work. They need separate CVs.

### Layer 0 · Conditions

Göteborg. Aliant states explicitly that remote work is not possible; Volvo Cars and A-Society are hybrid. Aliant is 6 months initially with extension, 100%. Ads split between Swedish and English — Aliant and A-Society are Swedish throughout, which is a signal about the CV language.

### Layer 1 · Gates

| Gate | Weight | Proof needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong production C++, 5+ years hands-on | 6/7 | Owned, on shipped code |
| Embedded Linux | 5/7 | Applied |
| Automotive industry experience | 7/7 | Named OEM or Tier-1 |
| University degree in Computer Science or related | 3/7 | Named |
| Senior technical role held before: tech lead, principal engineer, architect | 3/7 | Owned, with the title |

### Layer 2 · Core

| Requirement | Weight | Proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Production C++ in large embedded systems, including code review and coding standards | 7/7 | Owned |
| Embedded Linux or POSIX-like environments; QNX, hypervisors, Android Automotive | 5/7 | Applied |
| AUTOSAR Classic and/or Adaptive — configuration and integration | 4/7 | Applied |
| Software architecture and application design in embedded environments | 5/7 | Owned |
| Test strategy you defined: unit test, Google Test, HIL, desktop execution, in-vehicle | 5/7 | Owned |
| CI/CD for embedded: Jenkins, CMake, Git, Gerrit, Bazel, Zuul, Docker | 5/7 | Applied |
| Vehicle communication and interfaces: CAN, Ethernet, SOME/IP, UDS diagnostics | 4/7 | Applied |
| Python for tooling, monitoring and algorithm evaluation | 4/7 | Applied |
| Requirement breakdown and cascading into system and software requirements | 4/7 | Owned |
| Hardware-in-hand work: test rigs, debugging on target, prototypes | 3/7 | Applied |
| Establishing coding standards and review practice for a team | 3/7 | Taught |

### Layer 3 · Differentiators

ADAS, sensor fusion, Kalman filters, trajectory planning, Euro NCAP protocols. Software-Defined Vehicle platforms and their interfaces to embedded control. Experience from both OEM and Tier-1 sides, and clarity about who owns what between them — WirelessCar asks for this directly. OTA, software deployment and variant/configuration management. Enterprise Architect, SystemWeaver, Elektra. **Development with agentic AI** — A-Society lists it under meriterande. Open-source contributions and side projects — Volvo Cars asks for these by name, which is unusual and worth answering concretely.

### Layer 4 · Domain & process

ISO 26262, ISO/SAE 21434, ASPICE, MISRA C++ 2023, static code analysis. Agile; Volvo Cars runs FMEA, TARA and HARA in early phase and expects you to initiate them. Deliverable vocabulary these ads use: functional requirements, system requirements, SW architecture, test architecture and plan, compliance documentation.

### Layer 5 · Behaviour

Aliant is the clearest: lead technical development and build consensus **without formal personnel responsibility**, challenge technical assumptions, balance technical quality against schedule and commercial reality, hold technical dialogue with customers. ECARX asks for comfort with ambiguity and creating clarity where the requirements have not settled. Volvo (ADAS) wants someone who mentors juniors and raises the team's coding practice. The common thread is authority earned technically rather than granted.

### Anti-signals

C++ claimed only from desktop or server contexts. No target hardware, rig or vehicle anywhere in the CV. AUTOSAR named but never configured or integrated. Architecture claimed with no artefact — no architecture document, no interface specification, no design review you led. For the WirelessCar advisory assignment specifically: a pure engineering CV with no gap analysis, roadmap or organisational recommendation to point at.

## Archetype C — Cloud Backend, DevOps & Developer Platform

**Built from 6 ads:** WirelessCar (Senior DevOps Developer), WirelessCar (Senior Java with AWS), Combitech (Developer Platform Engineer), ICtech (C++ backend, OpenShift migration), Volvo Cars (Central Execution Environment DevOps), Verama (Senior Data & Analytics Engineer, adjacent).

**One-line pitch:** *I build backend services and I run them in production — including the pipeline, the infrastructure and the 3 a.m. incident.*

This archetype has a fork in it. The language and cloud differ by client (Java + AWS at WirelessCar, C++ + Azure/OpenShift at ICtech, Python/TypeScript + Azure at Combitech), but the *shape* of the role is identical across all six: own the whole lifecycle, automate it, operate it. Build one CV around the shape and swap the stack line per application.

### Layer 0 · Conditions

Göteborg, hybrid. WirelessCar states at least 2 days per week in the office. Consultant assignments within an international engineering organisation. English throughout.

### Layer 1 · Gates

| Gate | Weight | Proof needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7+ years professional experience (5+ at ICtech) | 3/6 | Years visible |
| Academic degree in CS, Mathematics or Engineering | 2/6 | Named |
| One primary cloud, deep: AWS, or Azure / Azure Red Hat OpenShift | 6/6 | Owned, with named services |
| One primary language at production depth: Java, C++, or Python/TypeScript | 6/6 | Owned |
| Production operations experience, not just development | 4/6 | Owned |

### Layer 2 · Core

| Requirement | Weight | Proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Backend services: REST, OpenAPI/Swagger, microservices | 5/6 | Owned |
| Containers and orchestration: Docker, Kubernetes or OpenShift | 5/6 | Applied |
| CI/CD systems: Azure DevOps, GitHub Actions, Jenkins, Gerrit/Zuul | 6/6 | Owned |
| Infrastructure as Code: CloudFormation, AWS CDK, Terraform | 3/6 | Applied |
| Monitoring, logging, observability, alerting | 3/6 | Owned |
| Incident investigation, troubleshooting distributed systems, root cause analysis | 3/6 | Owned |
| Automation and scripting: Bash, PowerShell, Python | 5/6 | Applied |
| Git-based workflows and modern collaboration practice | 6/6 | Applied |
| Full-lifecycle ownership: requirements through maintenance and improvement | 4/6 | Owned |
| DevSecOps and secure-by-design | 2/6 | Applied |
| **AI in the software development process: LLMs, agents, MCP** | 3/6 | Owned — see below |

The last line is not a nice-to-have in this archetype. WirelessCar lists "AI (agentic coding)" among **required** skills alongside Java and Lambda. Combitech builds the entire role around rethinking how software is built in-house with LLMs, agents and MCP. That is two of six ads treating it as core, which is why it sits in Layer 2 rather than Layer 3.

### Layer 3 · Differentiators

Backstage or other internal developer platforms. Serverless: Lambda, API Gateway. NoSQL, Maven, TDD, \*nix. Message queues and asynchronous patterns (IBM MQ named by ICtech). Oracle. Node.js and front-end work at the API layer. Databricks, Apache Spark and lakehouse architecture if the Verama line is pursued. A mechanical engineering background with calculation or simulation exposure — ICtech names this as a genuine differentiator, which is unusual enough to be worth a line if it applies.

### Layer 4 · Domain & process

Agile ways of working, Scrum. Connected-vehicle services and automotive telemetry as the application domain in four of the six. Cloud migration from on-premise as a current programme at ICtech. Golden-path and developer-experience thinking at Combitech.

### Layer 5 · Behaviour

WirelessCar wants someone who "likes understanding how things work in production" and fixes things with an upstream focus — not an operations specialist, but a developer who refuses to throw code over the wall. Combitech wants someone who listens to colleagues' everyday problems and removes them, and who has the confidence to propose new solutions. Both ask for willingness to challenge existing ways of working. End-to-end ownership of a problem is stated in four of the six.

### Anti-signals

Cloud named without named services. CI/CD claimed with no pipeline you built. Development experience with no production exposure — these ads screen for it specifically. A stack list with no architecture or operational decision attached. For Combitech in particular: platform experience with no evidence anyone actually adopted what you built.

## Archetype D — Applied AI / Agentic Systems Engineer

**Built from 5 ads:** Konsult poolen (Backendutvecklare, Agentic AI), Volvo VOLGJP00043496 (LLM pipelines for engineering automation), Aurobay (AI Consultant, Finance & Automation), the Clinical & Product AI test entry, Combitech (Developer Platform Engineer, shared with C).

**One-line pitch:** *I build agents that do real work inside existing systems, and I get the organisation using them.*

### Layer 0 · Conditions

Göteborg. Fluent English; Aurobay stresses international, cross-cultural teams. All five are explicitly hands-on building roles. Konsult poolen says it outright: this is for someone who wants to build, not a purely strategic AI role. That sentence should shape the whole CV.

### Layer 1 · Gates

| Gate | Weight | Proof needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong Python | 4/5 | Owned |
| Demonstrable hands-on LLM / generative / agentic AI work — shipped, not studied | 5/5 | Owned or Taught |
| M.Sc. in Data Science, Computer Science or Software Engineering | 2/5 | Named |
| Several years of professional software development behind the AI work | 4/5 | Owned |
| Fluent English | 4/5 | — |

The second gate is the whole archetype. Every one of the five ads uses the word hands-on or an equivalent. A CV that presents AI as an interest, a course, or a strategic capability fails here even with everything else intact.

### Layer 2 · Core

| Requirement | Weight | Proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design and build AI agents that call tools and APIs and act on real systems | 5/5 | Owned |
| Deciding what an agent must be able to do and how it interacts with existing systems and data sources | 3/5 | Owned |
| LLM-based backend services in production | 4/5 | Owned |

| Data extraction, cleaning and structuring: SQL, Python, Power Query, BigQuery, ERP sources | 2/5 | Applied |
| Proof of concept → evaluation → prioritised roadmap → scale | 3/5 | Owned |
| Enabling other teams: configuring the platform for their use, defining practice, guardrails, training colleagues | 3/5 | Taught |
| Containerisation, cloud, CI, DevOps around the AI work | 3/5 | Applied |
| APIs — FastAPI or similar | 2/5 | Applied |
| Translating business and product needs into technical AI solutions | 4/5 | Owned |

### Layer 3 · Differentiators

MCP — named directly by Combitech. Copilot Studio, Google ADK, Google BigQuery. Automotive or ADAS domain, called "strongly meritorious" by Konsult poolen. Engineering-workflow domain knowledge: fault management, change requests, test and QA, technical support flows — Konsult poolen lists these as very valuable and they are unusually specific. Regulated AI: IEC 62304, ISO 13485, EU MDR, the EU AI Act, model governance. MLOps, NumPy, Pandas. Finance, controlling or FP&A background for the Aurobay line.

### Layer 4 · Domain & process

The application domains split cleanly: engineering workflow automation (Volvo, Konsult poolen, Combitech), finance process automation (Aurobay), regulated clinical software (the Clinical AI entry). Each wants the AI skill plus one domain. Pick the domain per application rather than claiming all three.

### Layer 5 · Behaviour

Self-driven and comfortable with ambiguity, named in four of the five. An experimental disposition: Volvo's ad says "you never say no at first — you try things out before deciding". Combitech wants someone who will rethink how software gets built. Aurobay wants pragmatism under ambiguity combined with domain understanding. These are not generic — they describe someone who prototypes to find out rather than specifies to be safe.

### Anti-signals

AI as a listed interest with no artefact anyone used. Strategy or advisory framing when the ad says build. Model names and frameworks listed with no system you put into production. Python claimed only as a scripting sideline.

### Note on evidence

This is the archetype where internal tooling work counts as first-class experience rather than a side project: a skill that generates on-brand documents and is used by colleagues, an agent service with a canonical state file, a connector rollout inside an organisation. Those are Owned and Taught grade evidence against Layer 2 lines that most candidates can only mark Named. It is worth auditing what already exists before writing this one.

## What all five share

Four things hold across every archetype and belong in a common base layer rather than being rewritten five times.

**Gothenburg, onsite or hybrid.** Not one of the 25 ads offers remote work, and Aliant states it is not possible. Location is a gate everywhere.

**Seniority is stated numerically.** Eight of the ads name a year count: 5, 7, 8, 10, 12. The CV has to make the number legible in seconds rather than leaving it to be added up from dates.

**Consulting shape.** Most of these are konsultuppdrag with a scope, a duration and a deliverable. The ads reward phrases like "integrate deliveries from others", "operate independently", "influence across organisational boundaries". Assignment-shaped entries — client, scope, duration, what was delivered — argue this better than employment-shaped ones.

**Language follows the ad.** Six of the 25 are written entirely in Swedish (Aliant, A-Society, Konsult poolen among them). Matching the ad's language is a cheap signal and currently a coin flip.

### The AI crossover

The most useful pattern in the data is that AI tooling has stopped being its own category and started appearing as a requirement inside roles that are not AI roles:

| Ad | Archetype | How AI appears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WirelessCar, Senior Java + AWS | C | "AI (agentic coding)" listed under **required skills** |
| Combitech, Developer Platform Engineer | C | Build with LLMs, agents and MCP — the premise of the role |
| A-Society, Senior C++ Embedded | B | Development with agentic AI, under meriterande |
| Aurobay, Senior Function Developer | A | GitHub Copilot as a natural part of the way of working, a plus |
| Volvo IC Tech, SI ICE Control | A | Physics-inspired AI/ML and AI-augmented engineering workflows |
| Volvo VOLGJP00043496 | D | The entire role, under an embedded title |

That is six ads across four of the five archetypes. The practical consequence: the AI evidence does not belong only in the Archetype D CV. One line of it belongs in A, B and C as well — placed as *how I work*, not as a separate skill — because in those ads it is a differentiator against candidates who match the core just as well.

This is also the one place where the profile has something most competing CVs will not: tooling built and adopted by colleagues, rather than courses taken.

## Filling these in

For each archetype, go line by line through Layers 1 and 2 and mark the proof grade you can actually support: Named, Applied, Owned, Taught. The pattern that comes out decides more than the content of any single CV.

- Gates mostly Owned → build the CV.
- A gate you can only mark Named → that is the screening failure, and no amount of rewriting Layer 2 fixes it.
- Core mostly Named → the archetype is aspirational rather than current. Worth knowing before spending a day on it.

The rule for every bullet that reaches the page: it must name the proof, not the skill. "Simulink" is Named. "Owned the thermal coordination function from requirement breakdown to vehicle calibration, Simulink to Embedded Coder, across two truck platforms" is Owned, and it answers four Layer 2 lines at once.

### Three things worth settling before writing five documents

**The rejection pattern argues against an even split.** Archetypes A and B absorbed roughly two-thirds of the 33 applications and account for nearly every rejection, including two batch waves on 5 and 19 October. Archetype D is the smallest cluster by ad count but it is the one visibly leaking into the other four, and the one where the existing evidence is strongest and most recent. Five equally weighted CVs would put the most effort where the results have been worst.

**Rejections this uniform may not be about the CV.** Twelve rejections landing on two dates looks like batch processing — an agency clearing a pipeline, or a screen you never reached a human past. If that is what happened, better-tailored CVs will not change the outcome, and finding out why those closed is worth more than the next document. Worth checking whether any feedback came back with them.

**Three identities are in play.** Applications appear under norberto.munoz, mo.chen and stefan.areback, and several of the same ads were submitted under more than one. If these archetypes are meant to serve all three consultants, the deliverable is five templates to be filled per person, not five finished CVs — which is a different piece of work and worth deciding now.

### A note on reusing this

The applications system already extracts requirements into its own schema — role, themes, conditions, and requirement lines tagged must with terms and a category — but only 5 of the 25 jobs have been through it. The model in this document is a superset of that schema: the weight and proof attributes and the gate/core/differentiator split are additions, the rest maps onto what is already stored. If these archetypes are worth keeping current, it would be straightforward to express them in that same shape and let new ads score against them automatically.
